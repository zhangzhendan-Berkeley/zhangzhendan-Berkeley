CS180 Project 1: Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii Collection

Author: Zhang Zhendan

Overview

In this project, I implemented an algorithm to automatically align and colorize the digitized glass plate images from the Prokudin-Gorskii collection. Each image contains three monochrome channels (Blue, Green, Red) stacked vertically. The task is to properly align them to reconstruct a full RGB color image.

Algorithm

Similarity Metrics

To compare how well two channels are aligned, I tested both Sum of Squared Differences (SSD) and Normalized Cross-Correlation (NCC).

$$SSD(I_1, I_2) = \sum_{x,y} \big( I_1(x,y) - I_2(x,y) \big)^2$$

$$ NCC(I_1, I_2) = \frac{\sum_{x,y} (I_1(x,y) - \mu_1)(I_2(x,y) - \mu_2)} {\sqrt{\sum_{x,y} (I_1(x,y) - \mu_1)^2} \cdot \sqrt{\sum_{x,y} (I_2(x,y) - \mu_2)^2}} $$

Here $\mu_1, \mu_2$ are the mean pixel values of the two images. SSD measures absolute difference (smaller is better), while NCC measures correlation (closer to 1 is better).

Border Cropping

Directly computing similarity over the full images gave poor results, because noisy or damaged edges dominated the metric. To fix this, I cropped ~30% of the borders before scoring.

Pyramid Alignment

Brute-force searching over large displacements is slow for high-resolution .tif files. To improve speed, I used a pyramid approach:

  1. Construct a pyramid by repeatedly downsampling the image by a factor of 2 until the smallest dimension < 64 pixels.
  2. Align the channels at the coarsest (smallest) level with a small search window.
  3. At each higher resolution, double the displacement estimate and refine it locally within ± search_range pixels.

This “coarse-to-fine” strategy reduces runtime from minutes to seconds while keeping high accuracy.

Edge-based NCC

For difficult images like Emir, intensity differences across channels caused NCC to fail. To address this, I computed similarity on Sobel edge maps instead of raw intensity:

$$E(x,y) = \sqrt{\Big(\frac{\partial I}{\partial x}\Big)^2 + \Big(\frac{\partial I}{\partial y}\Big)^2}$$

Then NCC was applied to these edge maps, making alignment robust to brightness differences.

Discussion

The main challenges were efficiency on large .tif images and robustness on difficult cases like Emir. Cropping and pyramids solved the speed issue, while Edge-NCC solved the robustness issue. Overall, the algorithm performs well across the dataset, even on tricky images.
